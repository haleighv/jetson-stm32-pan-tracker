Find pin 1 without opening the case

  1. The ribbon's red stripe marks the end of the socket with pins 1 and 2. Each wire goes to the same pin number at both ends, so numbering survives the "opposite" ribbon.
  2. Power the Jetson. Meter black lead on a known ground (a USB port shell, or J17 GND).
  3. Probe the two holes at the stripe end: 3.3 V = pin 1, 5 V = pin 2. The next hole along the 5 V row is pin 4, also 5 V.
  4. That tells you which row is odd. If your socket faces the other way from this drawing, press Flip rows.

Electrical notes

  • Everything on the header is 3.3 V logic. Never feed it 5 V.
  • Pins 3, 5, 27, 28 (I2C) go straight to the SoC, open-drain with pull-ups on the carrier (2.2 kΩ / 1.5 kΩ), ±2 mA.
  • All other signal pins pass through TXB0108 level shifters: very weak drivers. Fine into a logic analyzer or another chip's input; won't light an LED or drive a long wire well.
  • 3.3 V and 5 V pins are rated 1 A each.
  • Pin functions other than GPIO, UART1 and I2C need the pinmux set in jetson-io.
